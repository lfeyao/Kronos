## What can and cannot be inferred

WiredCurve has substantial qualifying turnover. A high return and high turnover
are compatible with multiple strategies: repeated directional trades, leveraged
position rotation, options trades, or other approaches. They do not identify
holding periods, execution method, market-making, leverage or a repeatable edge.
Dollar-P&L alone cannot separate skill from starting capital/exposure.

Aggregated P&L changes cannot identify a particular asset or directional bet.
Observed drawdown from an earlier snapshot could reflect open marks, trades,
leaderboard recomputation or timing. No attribution is made from such changes.
The leaders are selected survivors; copying their apparent risk without seeing
losers, liquidation history and account sizes introduces severe selection bias.

## Requirements for a defensible reconstruction

- Timestamped fills (instrument, side, quantity, price), fees/funding and equity
  history would permit holding-period, exposure, turnover and entry/exit analysis.
- A public self-disclosure must be verified as belonging to the same participant;
  do not connect aliases to individuals by name similarity.
- Repeated synchronized public score snapshots can test rough return-factor
  hypotheses, not recover unique trades. No periodic monitor was started.
- Even a convincing behavioral match must be evaluated after costs on fresh
  data and against the US product universe and $1,000 loss objective. Different
  jurisdictional access and fee tiers may make replication infeasible.

Outcome: leader behavior can inspire research hypotheses, but no trader's actual
strategy has been reverse engineered from the available public data. No orders,
registration, fund movements or communications with traders were made.
